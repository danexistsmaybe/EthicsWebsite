## Introduction
Across discussions around open source software, particularly from its proponents and contributers, the word *community* comes up quite frequently. Community is, in many ways, a utopian word, a word that invokes all of the most beautiful parts of social relations while somehow navigating the difficulties. At its most abstract, the quality of being open source represents, perhaps, the realization of a modern, serious effort towards that utopian ideal and an indication that technology can be useful, perhaps even essential, for getting there. Nevertheless, the first free and open source software (FOSS) was not written for with such grand intentions; the reality of open source software takes a much more specific and nuanced form; and critics of such an idea claim that it is unprofitable, vulnerable, and ultimately unsustainable. After providing an interactive history of FOSS, I will examine the most recent development in its timeline, generative AI. I will go on to provide analysis of FOSS from the perspectives of the two biggest stakeholders, consumers and developers. Finally, I will discuss the economic reality of FOSS, and I will then conclude with the notion of open source as a movement and an ideal, ending with an argument that FOSS provides a clear framework towards realizing our collective hopes as a society.
