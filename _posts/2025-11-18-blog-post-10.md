**Article**  
[ARTificial: Why Copyright Is Not the Right Policy Tool to Deal with Generative AI](https://yalelawjournal.org/forum/artificial-why-copyright-is-not-the-right-policy-tool-to-deal-with-generative-ai)

<hr style="background-color: #000; border: none; height: 2px">

In *ARTificial: Why Copyright Is Not the Right Policy Tool to Deal with Generative AI*, Micaela Mantegna argues that copyright is not the appropriate avenue for challenging the nonconsentual use of intellectual property in the training of large generative AI models. They begin by outlining the different types of generative AI, particularly text to image architectures, then move to the ethical issues surrounding AI, and finally conclude with their arguments, from multiple perspectives, as to why copyright is not effective.

## Authors Should Not Need to be Motivated by Profit
Though it is quite exhaustive, one thing that Mantegna's essay lacks is an alternative solution to the copyright system. She claims she will cover this in a follow up essay, but in reality, I don't think there is a solution. Under the rule of the government of the United States, issues of property, theft, and ownership are delegated and penalized by the law. At times, Mantegna's essay functions as a good critique of law within a free market, as many of her arguments go beyond copyright specifically and apply themselves to the law in general. For example, the legal system favors consolidated, profit-driven entities like big tech corporations over individuals who must invest surplus time and capital that they do not already have, and this is true for any element of intellectual property law. 

One of her main points is that making copyright law more strict harms the artistic ecosystem, which has historically benefited from intercommunication and a high degree of collaboration. This is true, and yet a free and open system of art cannot be sustained in a system where artists' primary motivation is to make a living.
