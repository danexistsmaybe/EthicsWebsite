### My Thoughts on Cultural Representation
In general, I come from a dominant, hegemonic position, culturally speaking: I am a white man living in the middle of Minnesota. Thus, for me, accurate cultural representation is more about being critical of historicity and conventional narratives of historical events: for example, a more accurate representation of the Thanksgiving holiday would not include harmful images of "noble savage" indigenous people that are ready and willing to give up their lands to the invading settlers. However, the US is a particularly diverse nation, so this is another factor that I would want to see represented in depictions of US culture. Spanish is spoken by almost 14% of the population, so a representative sample of depictions of US culture should include the Spanish language relatively frequently. So, in short, cultural representation means being critical of harmful cultural imaginings from dominant perspectives, and then accurate representation of the diverse (and discrete!) cultural signifiers that might exist in some population.

Building off of that, when evaluating AI model output, I would hope to see people like me (again, white man coming from a middle class Christian family) criticized and placed in diverse environments and situations. By criticized, I do not mean depicted as evil or wrong, but rather that I would like to see the conventional image of happy, put-together, successful, and well-adjusted white US Americans challenged at times. Moreover, I want to see people like me in non-homogeneous spaces, that is, among the rich and poor, among people of different faiths, and participating in many different cultural institutions.


### Qualitative vs Quantitative Evaluation of AI
There are some aspects of ethics that can only be evaluated through qualitative evaluations like the one in this case study. Elements of human relations like culture and nationhood cannot be fully expressed through numerical analysis, so it is intuitive that more individualized analysis must be done somehow. On the other hand, it is a lot more difficult to evaluate progress this way, since, without some kind of numerical basis, one cannot say that a new AI model is *more* or *less* representative than a previous version. I think, however, that this is simply an inherent challenge of this field, as there is no way to comprehensively evaluate representation numerically. 

### Outlook: Can AI representation Be Improved
AI is like a liberalist's very euphoric dream, for AI is all-inclusive: it takes every ethnicity, background, world view, etc., and blends them all up into a bunch of matrix multiplications such that all of it is included somewhere. Naturally, under such an equal-opportunity system, the most represented catagories take over. I have my doubts that AI can be made globally inclusive in the way the case study would like because I think it already is inclusive, it just can't account for the complex and emotional thought required for inclusion to result in any sort of accurate representation. In the article, a participant says, "most text prompts will have 'such a wide range of portrayals' that there will always be the question of 'Which lens are you using?'" which gets at why this is so difficult. The AI can be told, "use this Indian nationalist lens," or, "use this Kashmiri nationalist lens," or so on, but then is limited to that one perspective. It is not capable of truly balancing many of these perspectives without just taking some kind of attention-weighted average. 

Of course, even if the ideal is impossible, that doesn't mean developers shouldn't try to improve things as they stand now. I think the most direct way to respond to the concerns of the study is to better currate the training data. Being more careful and intentional with the quantitative representation of certain viewpoints in the data might go a long way, and, while this is probably infeasible for humans to do, I wonder if current, primitive AI can help us improve the next generation. This is not something I feel confident speaking on, but I do think that AI is much better at accurately labeling the content of some text than accurately representing an entire culture in a generated body of work. Therefore, I wonder if AI could do a rough analysis on the training data that exists in order to determine what perspectives are represented; or, maybe this would just lead to more slop.

### Placing Representation Within History
Encoding is not at odds with the fluidity of representation. This is easily demonstrated. Cultural representation and language are the same in that they are both semiotic systems, that is, systems of signage and communication. One quality of such systems is their transience, which is evident in both cultural representation and language, the former being obviously dynamic, and the latter being a system that forms new words, breaks its own rules, and generally lives and breathes as a social organism rather than a prescriptive grammar. AI's somewhat deft use of language, mainly its encoding of the fluid meaning of words, shows that we have found ways to encode dynamic systems. I suppose that this is mainly accomplished through learned encodings in high dimensional vector spaces. 

However, it is also true that cultural representation is probably a lot more complexly dynamic than language is. I cannot answer whether or not we are currently capable of encoding representation in this form, on our current hardware, and I also cannot say whether or not this goal is reachable in the near-term. One point that I can end with, however, is that media seems to have become more representative as it has become more easily contributed to; for example, Palestinians in Gaza have been able to represent themselves on TikTok (until Larry Ellison bought it) even though mainstream media would rather not give them a voice. I think that if the people of the world decide that they really want to be well-represented by AI, they must find a way to contribute to it more freely. Now, seizing the means of production (nationalizing big tech companies) seems like a great way to do this, but I'll leave that thought as the following open question.

### Discussion Question
**How can the training data of AI be made more equitable and open-source, all while keeping models stable and as free from hate as possible?**

Again, I think this question is important because history tells us that making technology more equitable and open-source tends to make it more representative. The exact way to do this, however, is more complicated, hence asking the question.
