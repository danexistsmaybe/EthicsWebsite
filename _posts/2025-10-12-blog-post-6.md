*How does the technology garbage impact the global climate environment?*

**Article**  
[From Mining to E-waste: The Environmental and Climate Justice Implications of the Electronics Hardware Life Cycle](https://mit-serc.pubpub.org/pub/w9ht6hue/release/5?readingCollection=ca73f7c0)

### Introduction
With every small town in Minnesota chomping at the data center bit, I hear a lot about the climate impact of computing: but, it's all mostly in regards to software. The AI conversation has gotten so large that it has eclipsed conversations about computing hardware, the footprint of which may be less intuitive because of all the factors involved. When it comes to high performance computing, the climate impact can often be summed up in a few numbers: watts of energy burnt per hour, or gallons of water slurped every day, for example. The impact of hardware is just as large but more nuanced, the difficulties of which this case study seeks to explore.

It's not like machine learning isn't about hardware, either: some would say that the AI boom was due more in part to Nvidia's development of research-grade GPUs than to anything OpenAI ever did. Now, every research institution and data company wants their own RTX 6000, so what is the cost of all that? The article examines this issue through three different contexts: mining, socio-economics, and e-waste; these three broad categories cover the entire lifecycle of a computer chip, from its smog-ridden birth to its garbage-heap grave. It also focuses on developing the life cycle assessment (LCA) model for the impact of hardware, in order to build a standardized way of investigating this issue.
