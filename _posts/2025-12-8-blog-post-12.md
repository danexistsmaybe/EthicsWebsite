I assume that as the nature of this blog post is reflection, answering the reflection questions one by one is the expectation. Thus, without further ado: a semester of blog posts. 

## Superlative Round # 1: Most Incomplete Blog
If you visit [blog #8](/posts/2025/23/blog-post-8/), you will notice that there is not very much there. That is why I am calling blog #8 my most incomplete blog. This blog was born of the fact that it just doesn't seem worth it to go through a lengthy revision process just to get half credit for a due date one forgot about. Perhaps the craziest thing is that I remember reading that article, which means I've read all eleven blog post case studies for the semester. 

## Superlative Round # 2: Most Percolating Blog
I don't remember the earlier blogs so well. Thus, I'm saying the blog that made me think the most was blog #9, the one about censorship. I didn't like that blog so much, but it did really make me think about right and center wing populism, as well as some interesting stuff about epistimelogy. Perhaps it demonstrates that a bad article can be just as useful as -- or even more useful than -- one that simply says a bunch of stuff the reader is likely to agree with. Or, maybe I was just feeling particularly energetic that night. Either way, this was the most percolating blog of recent weeks.

## Revisions
The only blog I submitted revisions for was blog #3, and I mostly just had to change a few of the titles & move stuff around for that one. I think these changes reflect me getting more used to the blog format, which I've gone on to use for a different class' final project. Overall, my relative lack of total revision reflects the way in which I much prefer to get things done all at once, rather than having them linger, hanging over me like some Dolomedes wharf spider, waiting to trap me in a sticky web of homework the moment I stumble. So, I doubt I did particularly well on each assignment, I just decided to put in more work up front rather than go through a process of refinement.

## Writing Skills
I think I improved one writing skill in this course: writing long-form, casual, and slightly more compelling content. Blogs are not a medium that I have had a lot of practice with, so it was good to get more comfortable working in that format. Otherwise, I don't feel like my writing ability changed much. I think that doing a smaller number of more intensive assignments would have been more affective in that regard. In my English classes, any sort of assignment that happens as frequently as once a week is typically regarded as intended to get ideas on the page, and our writing is mostly only graded on its technical correctness (proper English).

## Blogs vs Essays
I expect that most of the students in this class prefer the blog format, and I don't necessarily disagree. Nevertheless, an academic blog and the sort of blog you might see about your favorite celebrity are not the same. As a medium, I think the blog has a lot of potential for making STEM students feel more comfortable writing, while still requiring some deeper thoughts on how to match up form and content. My opinion is that assigning blog posts as if they were essays might be more effective: that is, having fewer, more in-depth blog assignments, which are to be read/graded with more strict attention to rhetoric, style, and clarity. 

In fact, I think it's possible to get the best of every world: weekly writing assignments that are just 200-300 words about the weekly reading, and then 2-4 serious academic blog posts throughout the course of the semester, which might involve a little extra reading/research and more strict language expectations.

## Habits & Patterns
Every blog felt like I was winging it. None of them were outlined, researched, or methodical. At some point I realized that wasn't going to change. On the other hand, I think I was able to relax my style more throughout the semester, which is a good thing, because a hastily written paper reads very poorly, but a hastily written casual piece of writing reads at least a little more normal. Still, I can't help but take these authors pretty seriously. And, all of my posts are heavy discussion or argument and less interested in aesthetics, which is less than ideal because a blog is meant to be somewhat entertaining, I think. All in all, once I found a format/voice that worked -- that is, got me the grade -- there was little motivation for me to change it, because why risk having to do a revision?

## Topics & Themes
I most enjoyed writing about social and political topics, such as intellectual property, surveillance, or social media. I think that it would have been useful to talk more about how technology has affected economics in the last decade, which might include subscription models, crypto-currency, or online gambling. I liked finding at least one of my own resources to write about, but I think it requires more time to work through it. As things were, it just felt like a lot to have to find an article, read it, and write about it coherently in a small amount of time. 

## Knowledge & Themes
I actually felt like the connection between what we read for these blogs and what were studying in class was pretty weak. I think they're both useful, it just often felt like I was doing one thing outside of class and another inside of class. It might have helped to have class discussions about the readings, with specific questions that direct the readings to the curriculum's content. 

## Fate of this Website
I will not be using this website beyond this class. I already had a personal website that I think is better. In fact, it would have been convenient for me if I could have just posted these blogs to that website instead of this one. Nonetheless, it is not difficult to transfer them over, and I may do that over break. I will continue to post written assignments as well, even though they probably won't be blogs.

## Fate of the Writer
I will be taking a Fiction Writing course next semester, which will be my first experience writing fiction for a class. If I go to graduate school, I will write academic research papers, and maybe one day I'll write in a human-computer interaction context. I'm not so interested in blog posts, however.

## Writing Goals
Writing is not something that I feel is necessary for me or my life. I mean, I hope that I will always continue to write in professional contexts, but I would be okay with mostly just reading. That is to say, I don't feel the need to set any formal goals. Even so, I believe I will continue to write, even if its just the occasional poem.
